# Piggyback benchmark

180 simulated scenarios (seeds 1–20 × capacity tightness 0.3, 0.6, 0.9 × anomaly rate 0.02, 0.04, 0.08), 7 recovery policies, each plan replayed against the simulator's ground truth (real parcel locations, real trip delays). Generated by `python -m benchmark.run`; every row in `benchmark.csv` is reproducible from its seed and knobs.

All policies get the same detections, so differences come from recovery alone. ± is a 95% confidence interval over scenarios. Bold deltas are significant (the interval excludes 0).

## Headline

- vs **Next service, FCFS (TMS auto-rebook)**: +4.3 pp stranded parcels on time (95% CI +3.6 to +5.0); lower realized penalty by 19.6% (95% CI -22.4% to -16.8%), better in 159/180 scenarios.
- vs **Next service + truck for late EXPRESS**: -4.7 pp stranded parcels on time (95% CI -5.5 to -3.9); lower realized penalty by 41.2% (95% CI -43.3% to -39.0%), better in 178/180 scenarios.

## Caveats

- These are results on Piggyback's own simulator. Its assumptions (whole-trip delays, sampled spare capacity, no knock-on effects between recovered parcels) favour no policy in particular, but real networks will differ.
- Realized penalty = money spent + ₹250 × priority weight (EXPRESS 3, STANDARD 2, ECONOMY 1) × hours late, with failed and unrecovered parcels charged 48 h late.
- Plan time includes generating options and, for CP-SAT, solving (2 threads, 10 s limit).
- Reactive policies (the next-service baselines and the reactive ablation) get a second chance: an AT_RISK parcel they ignored is rebooked by their own rules when it really strands, 2 h (the detection grace) after its late arrival. Without that, the proactive advantage would be overstated.
- The replay checks each parcel's plan against the truth but doesn't re-simulate the network, so a recovery can't delay other parcels.
